What to do when years of returns are missing.

Missing returns are stressful because they feel like one enormous problem. In practice, they are a series of years, records, and deadlines that need to be put in the right order.

Do not start with a panic estimate.

You may not owe what you think you owe, and the IRS may not have every deduction or piece of information available when it makes its own calculation. The useful first step is to identify the missing years and locate the income, expense, and filing information for each one.

Work from the oldest year forward.

The right sequence can depend on your tax history, notices, business records, and state filings. A qualified preparer can help build a filing plan and identify where transcripts or other records are needed. Be honest about what exists and what does not; the goal is to make a defensible, accurate filing, not a perfect memory.

Do not ignore a current collection deadline while assembling old records. Get appropriate professional advice promptly if a notice, levy, wage garnishment, or other urgent matter is involved.
